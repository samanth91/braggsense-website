The science of certainty

A grating in glass. A wavelength that never lies.

When broadband light meets a Fiber Bragg Grating, one narrow band is reflected. Strain or temperature changes the grating period. The reflected wavelength moves. That shift is the measurement.

How FBG works

Wavelength in. Structure out.

An FBG is a periodic modulation of refractive index written into the core of a single-mode fibre — typically a few millimetres long. It acts as a wavelength-selective mirror. Stretch the fibre and both the period and the index change; the Bragg wavelength shifts linearly.

  • Typical strain sensitivity on the order of 1 µε
  • Temperature from cryogenic to 300 °C with the right packaging
  • Dozens to hundreds of gratings multiplexed on one fibre
  • No electronics, no spark, no EMI at the sensing point
What we measure

One fibre. Many physical quantities.

Strain & microstrain

Static and dynamic. Fatigue, crack onset, load paths, and residual strain after manufacture.

Load & root moment

Calibrated from fibre strain and known lever arms — flapwise force and bending moment on a blade root.

Twist & shape

Leading/trailing-edge couples and shear-web differentials reconstruct torsion along the span.

Temperature

Millikelvin-class stability for compensation and for process or cable hot-spot monitoring.

Vibration

High-rate interrogation for modal analysis, impact, and rotating machinery.

Damage index

Cycle-aware models that turn peak strain and load history into remaining useful life cues.

Why not a strain gauge?

FBG survives where copper does not.

Electromagnetic immunity

Install on high-voltage equipment, in radar fields, and through lightning-prone blades. The sensor is glass.

Massive multiplexing

One cable, many sensors, kilometres of reach. Installation mass and cost collapse versus point gauges.

Embeddable for life

Laminate into GFRP/CFRP or concrete. The grating does not corrode and does not need recalibration campaigns.

Intrinsically safe

No electrical energy at the measurement location. Suitable for hydrogen, oil & gas, and ATEX environments.
